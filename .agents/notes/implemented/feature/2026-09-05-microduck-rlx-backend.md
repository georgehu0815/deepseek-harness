# Agent Note: Explicit RLX training for MicroDuck

Status: implemented

English | [中文](2026-09-05-microduck-rlx-backend.zh.md)

## Problem

MicroDuck training needs an identifiable RLX execution path without changing the meaning of existing DSH-owned MLX experiments. An external dirty checkout cannot be identified by its Git revision alone. Exported policies can also agree with a rounded checkpoint normalizer while differing from the controller used during training.

## Decision

The [MicroDuck provider](../../../../packages/robot/robot-lab-microduck/README.md) offers `rlx` independently of `cpu` and `mlx`. Deployment configuration supplies both `rlxPythonBin` and `rlxSourceRoot`; `rlxPpo` resolves numerical inputs before admission. Requests cannot choose interpreter or source paths, and an unavailable learner never falls back to another backend. RLX owns PPO, its model and observation adapter; Harness owns session authorization, confined process supervision and artifact admission. Metal computes the policy and critic while CPU workers integrate MuJoCo physics.

The frozen recipe binds RLX source-content fingerprints, including dirty Python files and dependency declarations, plus requested and whole-rollout collected steps. Episode limits round up using the installed simulator's control interval, retaining requested duration, interval, whole-step count and effective duration so non-integral clip periods cannot shorten the motion. Native RLX timeout correction bootstraps the terminal-state value, stops the advantage trace at every reset and normalizes terminal observations without updating statistics twice. True terminations bootstrap zero. Raw Gaussian actions retain their corresponding log probabilities; actuator clipping does not justify clipping exported raw policy means.

The exporter retains RLX's actor graph but uses the live adapter's full-precision observation statistics and float64 arithmetic before its float32 actor input. CPU ONNX Runtime must match that controller, not only the checkpoint's rounded normalizer. Completed format-3 RLX runs bind the checkpoint, JSON metadata, full-precision normalizer, parity report and ONNX through the artifact manifest digest in `artifactSha256`. Missing or changed members prevent loading. Completion repeats frozen source, helper, model, runtime, BAM and clip validation after export and artifact verification, before committing the completed state. Successful export cannot publish a policy against changed scientific inputs. Numeric snapshots are inference evidence, not resumable optimizer or environment state.

Optional RLX observations separate completed collection/update intervals and checkpoint/export work from policy approval. They retain the last fully completed update's already-computed weighted total objective rather than inventing individual loss components. Collection includes inference and environment calls; update includes deferred critic and optimizer work, so neither is an isolated device-cost measurement. Counters and timings exclude partial work in unfinished or failed phases: optimizer steps applied before an update fails are not added to the reported count. Zero therefore does not prove no optimizer work occurred. Absent statistics are unknown and the training elapsed clock excludes checkpoint/export. This supports inspection without changing scoring semantics or converting a successful serialization into checkpoint-resume support. [The progress reference](../../../../docs/subsystems/robot.md#run-progress) owns the exact fields.

Training diagnostics retain completed episode lengths and right-censored tails separately: ending collection must not turn unfinished episodes into observed terminations or completions. Reference-index exposure is inferred from zero-start episode ages and the owned runtime reference, not observed policy phase or tracking. Completed runs bind canonical diagnostics through the existing checkpoint-metadata hashes, whereas raw progress prefixes have no policy binding. The public progress projection and assessment criteria remain independent.

The [Studio proposal](../../proposed/feature/2026-09-04-microduck-studio.md) remains active for its broader authoring, scientific-runtime and hardware requirements. Its DSH-owned `mlx` decision remains applicable to that backend; this note does not replace it. The [client guide](../../../../packages/client/ui-robot-lab/README.md) adds phase-specific next-step explanations and optional assessment tuning to existing controls, not a complete beginner curriculum. Studio provides explicit session creation or selection and [blank-session presentation](../architecture/2026-09-05-blank-session-presentation-views.md) without chat; [Conversation-owned selection](../architecture/2026-09-05-conversation-owned-view-commands.md) takes successful Studio Start/Open to Micro Duck, while creating a session does not save a target or start training.

## Alternatives considered

**Reinterpret `mlx` as RLX.** Existing manifests identify a different PPO implementation. Reusing that value would hide the trainer change and weaken reproducibility.

**Copy PPO into Harness or parse the example's console output.** Both create another algorithm or unstable integration API to maintain. The adapter imports RLX and publishes structured progress through the existing supervisor. Progress is observation, not completion authority: terminal state and policy identity remain authoritative even when the retained progress sample is null or trails completion.

**Accept checkpoint-to-ONNX agreement alone.** Matching float32-rounded statistics in both paths can conceal divergence from training-time normalization. Publication requires the live-normalizer comparison and retains its evidence.

## Consequences

Users need an explicitly installed Python 3.12 RLX environment on Apple Silicon with working Metal. The provider does not install dependencies or modify external checkouts. Source and helper changes can block replay; retain complete run artifacts and the matching runtime rather than rewriting provenance. Neither optional GPU learner promises faster or better learning than CPU SB3.

A bounded bridge smoke exercises a four-step saved Head Bob run and an eight-step timeout run, including training, export, policy reload, recorded simulation, assessment and blocked deployment. Its live-normalizer parity reports maximum absolute error below `6e-7` against tolerances `rtol=1e-4`, `atol=1e-5`. These are integration measurements, not evidence of a learned dance. A separate 32-step [Loader-owned provider test](../../../../packages/robot/robot-lab-microduck/tests/provider.e2e.ts) verifies source-mode RLX execution with confined CPU physics, frozen artifact identity, simulation, evaluation and blocked deployment. The [real lifecycle test](../../../../packages/robot/robot-lab-microduck/tests/lifecycle.e2e.ts) verifies ordinary Stop and Stop after owned worker failure, no surviving owned processes or final policies, and capacity reuse by a fresh completed run; it does not establish automatic recovery or checkpoint resume. These checks and a successful build do not establish the authenticated built-browser workflow, which requires separate verification and human review. The [focused helper tests](../../../../packages/robot/robot-lab-microduck/tests/test_rlx_ppo.py) cover configuration, source identity, artifact binding and export arithmetic; the [bounded smoke](../../../../packages/robot/robot-lab-microduck/tests/smoke_rlx.py) owns the real bridge exercise.

[Frozen choreography assessment](2026-09-05-microduck-frozen-choreography-assessment.md) supplies optional experimental criteria independently of learner choice. Checkpoint resume, live controller-state streaming, calibrated general choreography qualification and physical activation remain unavailable. Optional training observations do not supply transfer/memory measurements, warmup attribution or cold-versus-warm cost comparisons. Historical upright criteria and a training-completed label cannot certify dance completion or robot safety.
